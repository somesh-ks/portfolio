I wanted to read a few favourite novels as comics, and none existed. So I built a pipeline that does the boring parts of adaptation, splitting chapters into beats and beats into panels, and leaves the judgement to me.

## The problem

- Prose has no panels. A paragraph can hold three actions or none.
- Characters drift. A jacket changes colour between pages, a scar moves sides.
- Generated images look impressive alone and fall apart in sequence.

<BeforeAfterScrubber variant="comic" />

## Three bets

### Structure before pictures

The pipeline first turns a chapter into a blueprint: beats, panels per page, shot type and who is in frame. Nothing is drawn until the blueprint reads well as a page.

### Continuity as data

Every character and place gets a sheet: what they look like, what they wear, what never changes. Each panel prompt is built from the sheet, not from memory, so the jacket stays blue.

### The spread is the unit

Comics are read a page at a time, so the tool lays out and reviews spreads, not single images. A panel that works alone but breaks the rhythm of the page gets redone.

## Tradeoffs

- Blueprints are slow to review, but they are where adaptation actually happens.
- Strict continuity sheets make images more consistent and a little less surprising.
- I only adapt books for my own shelf, so the PDFs stay private.

## Other fun stuff

The hardest panel so far was a character thinking. Prose does that in one line. A comic needs a whole page.
