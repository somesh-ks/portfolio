I wanted to read dense classics from _Paradise Lost_ to _Frankenstein_ as comics, without wading through walls of archaic prose. A stalled single-book experiment grew into `sk-comics`, a reusable press where one prompt draws an entire lettered page.

<ComicEditionReader />

## Why sixty pages drift

- Characters shed continuity across pages: coats change color, scars switch cheeks, and faces age ten years between scenes.
- Unconstrained covers default to glossy movie posters, wiping out book trade dress and period serif typography.
- Proper nouns and 15-plus-word speech balloons garble into spellings like `"DALLIWEY"` and scramble panel pacing.

## Locking ink and face

Before a panel renders, the press synthesizes a period medium and a five-pigment hex gamut for the book. A master style anchor locks line weight, paper tooth, deep focus without lens blur, and a balloon lettering key. Recurring cast members get turnaround sheets so faces and coats hold across chapters.

## Blueprint before ink

On disk, `pages_manifest.json` plans the whole book before the image model draws a line. It maps panel transitions, sets panel weights, clears top-third sky bands for balloons, and caps dialogue at 12 words per balloon.

## Waves and gates

Batching 20-page concurrent waves on Flora MCP with disk-compiled manifests shrank 58-minute end-to-end runs by more than 6x. Text-only Jev has no eyes, so QA splits honestly: a vision pass transcribes rendered balloons for Jev to proofread against the script, single-second Jev batches check beat coverage, fidelity, and pacing monotony, and 12-up contact sheets catch visual drift by eye.

## Wrong format

Adapting Percy Shelley's 14-line _Ozymandias_ into a 35-page narrative comic stretched the sonnet thin and flattened its irony. Scrapping story panels for a 14-plate steel-engraved visual essay taught the press when a book wants diagrams more than balloons.

## Looking back

- Strict five-pigment locks and 12-word balloons trade happy accidents for whole-book coherence, and the pages read better for it.
- Baking lettering into the artwork on GPT Image 2.5 Flare avoids pasted-on PDF text overlays, though faster engines with high typo rates still need a separate text layer.

Full circle: I ended up running OCR on Scott McCloud's _Understanding Comics_ to critique sequential art with the same rules that built the press.
