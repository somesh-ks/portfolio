I wanted to read a few favourite novels as comics, and none existed. So I built a pipeline for the boring parts of adaptation and kept the judgement for myself.

## The problem

- Prose has no panels. A paragraph can hold three actions or none.
- Characters drift. A jacket changes colour, a scar moves sides.
- Generated images look great alone and fall apart in sequence.

<BeforeAfterScrubber variant="comic" />

## Three bets

### Structure before pictures

Each chapter becomes a blueprint first: beats, panels per page, shot type, who is in frame. Nothing is drawn until the page reads well.

### Continuity as data

Every character and place gets a sheet of what never changes. Panel prompts are built from the sheet, so the jacket stays blue.

### The spread is the unit

Comics are read a page at a time, so I review spreads, not single images.

<ComicStageScrubber />

## Tradeoffs

- Blueprints are slow to review, but that's where adaptation happens.
- Strict sheets make images consistent and a little less surprising.
