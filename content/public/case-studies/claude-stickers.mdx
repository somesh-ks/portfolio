The stickers started as a gift for the Claude community in Sweden and turned into a small production line. Each sticker is a grid of pixels with a few frames of animation, and each one had to look right in Slack, in Keynote, on a website and printed on a laptop lid.

## The problem

- A pixel that is off by one is invisible at 2x and obvious at 8x. Hand-drawn frames drifted between exports.
- Every format had its own quirks: GIF has no partial transparency, Lottie wants vectors, slides want video.
- Checking 40 frames by eye did not scale, and I kept missing the same mistakes.

## Three bets

### One model, many outputs

Every sticker is stored as rectangles on a grid, not as an image. Exporters read the model and write SVG, Lottie, GIF or a `<claude-mascot>` element. A fix in the model fixes every format at once.

### QA at the cell level

A small checker compares every frame against the grid and flags cells that are half-filled, off-palette or out of bounds. It caught more mistakes in its first run than I had found in a week of looking.

### Let people play with it

A sticker you can poke is more memorable than one you look at. The sheet below is the real set, rendered live from the same model.

## Tradeoffs

- Rects make shading hard, so the style stays flat. I chose that look on purpose, but it rules out some ideas.
- GIF export dithers semi-transparent edges, so stickers have hard edges everywhere.
- Writing exporters took longer than drawing. It paid off on the third format.

## Other fun stuff

The first person to print one put it on a water bottle, which is a format I did not plan for.
